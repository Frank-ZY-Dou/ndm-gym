# Ablations: which factor contributes what

SuperTrack (ST) and SuperMimic (SM) on the shared pipeline
([how the numbers were obtained](results.md#how-the-numbers-were-obtained)). Three kinds of evidence, from weakest to
strongest:

1. **Preliminary, policy:** pairs of tuning runs (seed 0, one run each, 3 h) that differ in a single factor, compared at
   iteration 10000 (equal iterations, hence equal simulator steps) and at the 3 h checkpoint. Runs started before the
   faster forward kinematics (rounds 1 and 2) reach fewer iterations in 3 h, so the 3 h column mixes the factor with
   that speed difference; the 10000-iteration column does not. A single-seed difference counts only when it exceeds
   twice the standard deviation of the difference of two single-seed runs, from the seed-to-seed spread of the final
   round (bold in the tables).
2. **Offline world-model benchmark** (`ndm_gym.wmbench`): every variant fit on the same fixed datasets and scored on
   the same test windows; three fits per variant (seeds 0-2) and three base fits.
3. **Ablation study:** one factor removed at a time from each full configuration, seeds 1-3, three standard evaluations
   per final checkpoint plus push tests, Welch 95% confidence intervals.

The tables are generated by `python tools/ablations.py --logs logs --wmbench results/wmbench --results results/abl results/final --md benchmarks/g1_lafan1_tracking/ablations.md`.

## Preliminary findings

From the single-seed tuning runs (table A) and round 3 (table B). Where the leave-one-out study covers a factor (table
D), its result holds. It confirms that the failure penalty trades MPJPE for fewer failed clips and that the
narrower reward kernels help SuperMimic, and it finds no significant effect of the balance weights with the failure
penalty at 5.

**SuperTrack.** The failure penalty is the main factor behind SuperTrack's fewer failed clips: from 0 to 1 it raises the return by 0.09-0.11 and
removes 6-9 failed clips (MPJPE-local -0.3 mm at 10000 iterations, +2.1 mm at 3 h), and from 1 to 3 it removes 2-5
more at 2.3-2.9 mm of MPJPE-local. Weighting the balance terms by 4 removes another 3-4 failed clips at penalty 1
without costing precision (MPJPE -1.7 mm at 3 h) but adds 0.1-0.3 deg of joint-angle error. Going from penalty 3 to 5 is within seed noise at 3 h and worse at 10000
iterations, so the selected penalty 5 is not distinguishable from 3. The task penalties cost precision in the tuning pair (MPJPE
+5.1 mm at 10000 iterations, +1.9 mm at 3 h; joint angle +0.4 deg) and make the actions smoother (action rate -2.6 to
-2.8); in the study, training without them is unstable (table D). Pre-failure imagination starts, imagination kicks,
more simulator noise and a tighter termination margin all cost MPJPE (1.5-8.8 mm, at one or both points) without a
robust gain; pre-failure world-model windows changed nothing beyond noise. All tuning together: return
+0.12 to +0.14 and 11-12 fewer failed clips, paid with 3.6-6.8 mm of MPJPE-local and 0.5 deg of joint-angle error.

**SuperMimic.** Narrower reward kernels (x0.7) make it more precise (MPJPE -1.8 mm at 10000 iterations, -4.6 mm at
3 h; joint angle -0.5 to -0.7 deg) at unchanged return; x0.5 is as precise, with a return 0.02 lower (within seed
noise) and 3 more failed clips at 10000 iterations. Simulator noise 0.3 has no consistent effect (MPJPE +1.7 mm at 10000
iterations, -0.7 mm at 3 h); imagination kicks cost 4.3 and 1.6 mm of MPJPE. Their benefit is robustness to pushes, which the
tuning runs did not measure: in the study, SuperMimic without kicks fails 4.3 / 9.3 more clips under 1 / 2 m/s pushes
(table D). Less simulator noise (0.05) is more precise.
Pre-failure imagination starts and a discount of 0.995 lower both return and precision. All tuning together changed the return by +0.004
and MPJPE by -2.4 mm at 3 h.

**World model (offline; table C, three fits per variant unless noted).** More data of the same policy helps most: -22%
in distribution, -19% out of it. Data collected with action noise 0.3 gives no significant change; its three fits
differ widely (ST-WM -16% / -17%, SM-WM +2% / -2%). Removing LayerNorm lowers the error by 16-17% in distribution and
14% out of it, for both world models. On the same data SM-WM errs 13% more than ST-WM in distribution and 11% more out
of it. The RigidFormer backbone at the default learning rate helps ST-WM out of distribution (-11%) but not
significantly in distribution (-4%), and changes SM-WM by no significant amount (+8% / +0%); with the learning rate
used online (4e-4) ST-WM is 16% better in distribution and 21% out of it, SM-WM 7% and 11%, all significant. L2 window loss
(+7% / +5%) and pre-failure windows (+6 to +8% / +5 to +7%) are worse; error-prioritized windows, input noise, four
integration substeps and the GELU, ELU and Mish activations change nothing significant (-3 to +2%). Training on the
other policy's data costs 22% on the first policy's test set.
